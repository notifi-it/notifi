PROFILE PICTURE — all four platforms

  docs/socials/images/profile.png
  1024x1024, the mark alone on the app icon's grain plate.

  Same surface as the App Store icon, at the same seed and blend, because the
  listing and the avatar get seen together and a flat one beside a grained one
  looks like two apps. The grain survives the downscale and re-encode a platform
  puts an avatar through — checked at 176px, JPEG q70.

  Not the lockup: every platform crops the avatar to a circle, which takes the
  ends off a word before it takes anything off a bell.

  X            400x400 displayed, accepts up to 2MB
  Instagram    320x320 displayed
  Facebook     176x176 displayed on desktop, 196x196 on mobile
  LinkedIn     300x300, 4MB max


BANNER / COVER

  docs/socials/images/x-banner.png          1500x500
  docs/socials/images/facebook-cover.png    1640x856
  docs/socials/images/linkedin-banner.png   1128x191

  Bell and wordmark centred on the same grain plate as the avatar, nothing
  else. The bell's badge is white here, not red: the wordmark ends in a dotless
  `i` whose tittle is already brand red, and two red discs side by side read as
  two dots competing rather than one idea. The avatar keeps the red badge,
  because standing alone it is the only thing making the silhouette an `i`.

  A tagline was left out on purpose: X crops the banner's bottom behind the
  avatar, Facebook crops the sides, and a line of type that survives all three
  has to be small enough that nobody reads it anyway. The bio underneath each
  banner already says it.

  Facebook is the awkward one. The file is 1640x856, but most people see a
  640x360 crop out of the middle, so the lockup is sized against that box rather
  than against the file. Anything outside the middle is decoration for desktop.

  Instagram has no banner.


INSTAGRAM MOSAIC

  docs/socials/images/instagram-grid/post-1.png … post-6.png   1080x1350 each
  docs/socials/images/instagram-grid/preview.png               not for posting

  One bell, whole and uncropped, cut into six across three columns and two rows.
  Two rectangles are in play and the gap between them is the whole trick.

  A feed post is 4:5, 1080x1350. A profile grid thumbnail is 3:4 — measured off
  a live profile at 279x372, since Instagram changed the grid from square in
  early 2025. The grid fills its frame from the post, so it shaves 6.25% off the
  width, about 33px a side.

  So the picture is drawn at the width that survives that crop — three 1013px
  cells, 3039 total — and each cell is centred in a 1080-wide post. The 33px of
  plate either side is exactly what the grid discards, which is what keeps the
  bell continuous across the seams. Draw at the post width instead and the grid
  eats a strip out of every join.

  The padding is plate, not white. It is cropped away in the grid but fully
  visible in the feed, where white bars down both sides of a dark tile read as a
  mistake.

  Two rows, not three. The bell is very nearly square and three rows of 4:5
  cells are not: fitted whole into 3x3 the mark spans about two thirds of the
  height and the rest is bare plate in two bands, so the top and bottom rows
  carry almost nothing. Two rows come to 3039x2700, close enough to the mark's
  own proportions that fitting it whole fills the frame.

  The plate carries extra grain here and nowhere else. It cannot be had by
  raising the 22% blend — that mixes toward mid-grey, so more of it is a lighter
  background rather than a grainier one. A grey noise layer is laid over in
  Overlay instead, which leaves the tone alone and only widens the spread. The
  avatar keeps 22% because it is matched to the App Store icon on purpose.

  Post them in filename order, 1 through 6. Instagram fills the grid newest
  first from the top left, so the numbering is already the reverse of the
  reading order: post-1 ends up bottom-right, post-6 top-left. Posting them in
  the wrong order mirrors the image, and the only fix is deleting posts.

  The grid only resolves once all six are up, and it breaks the moment a seventh
  post lands unless it starts a fresh row of three.
